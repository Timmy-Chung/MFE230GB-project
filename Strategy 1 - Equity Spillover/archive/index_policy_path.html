Policy Path and Terms of Trade

Two identified trading strategies for Currency Markets. The first trades G10 FX and duration around policy-path information. The second trades emerging-market NDFs around oil terms-of-trade shocks. Prediction markets are the alternative data; the economic logic is Topics 3–5, not a Sharpe hunt.

One theme, two books

At horizons under a year, exchange rates are mostly demand and information flow, not scheduled macro. We trade that claim in two universes that fail in different weeks.

Strategy 1 · OECD · Policy Path

G10 FX forwards, vol-targeted, with a VIX crash filter and a policy-path overlay from 2-year yields and Polymarket P(cut). Carry is harvested only when global risk is quiet — raw developed HML has not paid since 2010.

Universe EUR, JPY, GBP, AUD, CAD, CHF, SEK, NOK, NZD vs USD
Horizon weekly · 2 bp half-spread · 10% vol target on the combined book

Strategy 2 · EM · Terms of Trade

Dollar-controlled oil betas, estimated 2010–19 and frozen. Long the two highest-beta EM names, short the two lowest, timed on last week’s Brent sign. The mechanism is Topic 3; the trading friction is Topic 1 (NDF spreads).

Live names long BRL, THB · short ZAR, INR
Horizon weekly (mechanism) and monthly (live NDF) · 15 bp round-trip

Unconditional G10 carry is the wrong trade. Conditioning on global risk, and overlaying policy-path shocks, is the course-consistent one.

Our G10 book after 2 bp costs

Cross-sectional carry-to-vol, long 3 / short 3, plus a VIX filter (flat when VIX is above its expanding 80th percentile) plus a 2-year overlay: when the US 2-year rallies more than 15 bp, add long EUR+JPY and a duration sleeve. Combined Sharpe 0.62 (2000–2026; VIX-filtered carry alone 0.66), 0.84 out of sample 2020–26, max drawdown −19%. The book is in the market 83% of weeks with gross turnover of about 4.5× a year, so costs are roughly 9 bp a year. Corrected run of 7 Oct 2026: the monthly OECD rate is now carried across the weeks of the month (an earlier draft was flat three weeks in four and reported 0.35 / 0.57).

Terms of Trade

A 10% ToT improvement appreciates the real exchange rate (Topic 3). A static exporter–importer book does not pay. Timing the oil shock does — until you pay NDF costs every week.

What the costs say

Gross, oil-timed long BRL+THB / short ZAR+INR has Sharpe 0.28 versus 0.04 always-on (2010–2026). That is the mechanism test: the premium is for bearing the shock, not for sitting in BRL. The breakeven half-spread is about 4 bp (gross 4 bp a week against 0.98 units of turnover a week); charging the 15 bp NDF spread wipes the edge (net Sharpe −0.74). Monthly holding does not restore it. A placebo that shuffles the Brent signs beats 0.28 about 13% of the time, and the timed pair's residual dollar beta is 0.04. EM ToT is identifiable and not cheaply tradable at NDF spreads — which is what Topic 1’s 10–16 bp EM spread chart predicted.

Prediction markets

Polymarket Fed-decision contracts, December 2024 – January 2026. P(cut) is the sum of the 25 bp and 50+ bp decrease markets, averaged across live meetings.

Lead/lag vs the 2-year

Weekly ΔP(cut) correlates 0.42 with last week’s 2-year rally and 0.27 contemporaneously. In this sample the prediction market lags the 2s. That is the brief’s own falsifier: if the ΔP has already printed in 2-year yields, we are late. We therefore use 2-year changes as the long-sample policy-path shifter and treat Polymarket as the 2025–26 overlay and as a disagreement/confirmation series, not as a free look-ahead.

Short-sample overlay

Adding a EUR/JPY overlay when weekly ΔP(cut) exceeds +5 pp, on top of the VIX-filtered G10 book, produces Sharpe 1.18 from January 2025 to October 2026 (n = 92 weeks, 79 active); the overlay on its own is 0.67 on 57 weeks with 31 trigger weeks. That number is too small a sample to be a headline. It is reported as a live-period illustration, with the lead/lag result above attached.

When each book loses money

Policy Path

  • Carry crash. Developed HML: Oct 2008 −13.8%, Mar 1995 −10.4%, Oct 1998 −8.5%. The VIX filter is supposed to cut these; if it does not, the strategy is rejected.
  • Fiscal dominance. Yields up, currency down (UK 2022, Japan 2025–26). The duration overlay is then the wrong side.
  • Announcement catch-up. If PM ΔP only predicts inside the FOMC window, we are trading the statement, not order flow.

Terms of Trade

  • Oil crash. 13 Mar 2020 Brent −35%, BRL −2.6%, ZAR −4.3%. 20 Mar: oil −23%, BRL −4.9%, ZAR −7.0%.
  • Country risk. A Brazil or South Africa week can dominate oil. Single-name cap 35%.
  • NDF liquidity. 15 bp round-trip is enough to erase weekly timing. That is a result, not a footnote.
  • Dollar wave. Betas are estimated with dDollar on the right-hand side; residual dollar beta is the specification check.
