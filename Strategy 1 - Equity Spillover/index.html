Equity Spillover into FX

When a country's stock market beats the US, investors buy its assets and its currency follows. Each month, each currency: long against the dollar if its index beat the US over six months, short if it lagged. Equal weight, no leverage. Nine G10 currencies at 2 bp and nine floating EM currencies at 15 bp. The rule was chosen on 2000–2015 and judged on 2016–2026.

1 · The idea

Exchange rates are set by flows that are mostly latent, and demand is inelastic (Topic 5), so a modest cross-border equity flow moves the price. The dollar is the other side of most trades and moves with US equity outperformance (Topics 1 and 3), so the relevant question is relative: did this market beat the US, not did it go up. Hau and Rey's uncovered equity parity predicts the opposite sign (investors rebalance out of winners); the data say positive at a six-month horizon. "Beat the US" earns 0.41; "went up" earns −0.03; the signal used one month late earns 0.12, so the flow is absorbed within weeks.

2 · Constructions and selection

Two families. Cross-sectional: score every currency, rank them against each other, long the top, short the bottom; longs equal shorts, so no net dollar position. Four measures × four lookbacks × two weightings × two holding periods = 64 versions. Dollar-relative: no ranking; each currency judged against the US alone, so the book can be net long or short dollars. Six versions. Everything was ranked on 2000–2015 and reported on 2016–2026 untouched.

3 · The rule

Universe and signal

  • G10: AUD CAD CHF EUR GBP JPY NOK NZD SEK vs USD; spot plus the 3-month rate differential.
  • EM: BRL MXN ZAR CLP COP PLN HUF CZK KRW vs USD, the floating currencies per Topic 2; managed currencies (INR CNY IDR TRY) excluded because reserves absorb the flow.
  • Signal: six-month log return of the OECD share-price index in local currency minus the US index. Positive, long next month; negative, short.

Sizing, rebalance, costs

  • Every currency always in the book at 1/9 of gross; the signal only sets the sign. Gross 100%, no leverage, no vol targeting.
  • Month-end rebalance for the following month, using indices known at the close.
  • Half-spread per unit of turnover: 2 bp in G10, 15 bp in EM. Turnover 3.7× and 3.3× a year.
  • Two parameters, the 6-month lookback and the US benchmark, fixed on 2000–2015. 3- and 12-month lookbacks also hold (0.23 and 0.30 full sample in G10).

Current G10 book (2026-05-31): long JPY, CAD, NOK; short EUR, GBP, AUD, CHF, SEK, NZD. EM floats: long BRL, MXN, KRW, PLN, HUF, CLP; short ZAR, CZK, COP.

Ranking EM currencies against each other loses money in both halves. Judging each against the US holds in both. But the dollar component is the whole return: investors allocate to EM as an asset class, so the flow pushes all nine currencies together, and country-versus-country equity performance does not map to a country-specific flow. In EM this is a dollar trade. Managed currencies, for reference: Sharpe −0.38 out of sample.

No fat left tail: skew is zero or positive and the worst month is about one vol, so this is not carry. The cost is time, not depth: three years under water in G10 and five in EM, on an unlevered book earning under 2% a year. The books lose when the dollar jumps against the net position: 2009-01 (-5.2%), 2000-04 (-3.4%), 2022-09 (-3.2%), 2011-04 (-3.2%) in G10. The signal looks back six months and has no view on the dollar itself.
